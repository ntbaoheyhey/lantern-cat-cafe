# A1 — drawn keys over a looping idle
RA1 answer: use a single visible drawing track, discrete attachment changes, shared foot anchor and per-pose sockets. Keep the underlying idle available, but hide its attachments while a drawn key owns the silhouette. Use a brief directional smear around the K1-to-K2 cut, then a held contact; the presentation clock continues and the total action does not stretch. Exact smear/hold choice follows our experiment below.
RA2 answer: geometry/identity are checked before colour. Keep costume, outline, head scale, baseline and props fixed. Compare opaque masked Lab statistics and local palette appearance; retain alpha. Colour matching cannot repair changed anatomy. Use own-art conditioning; the IP-Adapter paper illustrates why image references help, not the internal architecture of our image tool.

## Professional method and evidence
1. Pose-to-pose staging makes intent readable before secondary movement (Lasseter).
2. One visible attachment prevents a ghosted double silhouette (Spine attachments).
3. Attachment keys and event keys share the same timeline (Spine keys).
4. Zero mix is available for specific transitions; interpolation is not mandatory for drawings (Spine mixing).
5. Backward scrubbing should sample, not accumulate animation state (Spine Timeline API; current drawing layer).
6. Freeze the contact pose locally; do not insert time into deterministic combat.
7. A smear is an authored directional bridge, not a crossfade of two faces (own drill).
8. Compare Lab mean/quantile transfer against no correction; Reinhard used l-alpha-beta, so our Lab implementation is explicitly an adaptation.
9. Decode painting colour once and use consistent texture alpha (Three colour management).
10. Validate identity and feet at battle size before broadening the roster (P2 results).

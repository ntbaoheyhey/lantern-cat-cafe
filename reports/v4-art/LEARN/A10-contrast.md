Study before production:
- [Hall and Hanna2004, paper](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/maltt/cosys-2/textes/hall04.pdf): their136-participant web-page experiment associates higher contrast with perceived readability, without a significant retention effect. Inference: separate tiny labels from textured art while retaining the palette. This does not validate a specific game or colour pair.
- [WCAG contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html): normal text needs4.5:1, large text3:1. Use4.5:1 for every scoped label, including responsive speed text; calculate with the0.04045sRGB threshold without rounding a failure up.
- [G18 measurement](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G18): inspect the background immediately behind glyphs. Authored colours and rendered backing are the evidence; antialiased edge shades are not separate foreground colours. A local plaque can isolate a glyph from media.
- [W3C compositing](https://www.w3.org/TR/compositing-1/): source-over alpha1 excludes underlying imagery. An opaque backing must cover the actual glyph range, including overhanging counters; an adjacent badge is insufficient.
- [MDN filter](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/filter): ordered filters matter. Drop-shadow adds a contour shadow; brightness changes both text and backing. Inspect hover separately and preserve uncertainty for unsupported ancestor opacity or grayscale.
- [WCAG non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html): meaningful control/state graphics need3:1. A text-only check does not establish every icon, focus outline or inactive/fallen state.

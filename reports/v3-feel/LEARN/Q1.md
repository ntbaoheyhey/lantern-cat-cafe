# Q1: one schedule, separate frame confirmation

Question: Are the remaining ultimate and overlap failures real scheduler defects, or comparisons between different clocks?

## How professionals handle this
- Keep one monotonic animation timeline for one simulation step (Q1-time, Q1-timer).
- Pass the RAF callback timestamp through update and render; callback completion time includes the work already performed (Q1-raf).
- Do not infer scheduled start by subtracting elapsed animation time from a post-draw timestamp (Q1-time, Q1-experiment).
- Model action boundaries and impact offsets explicitly; hold contact poses inside the fixed envelope (Q1-source).
- Check serial transition order in the scheduler itself, including round-status plans (Q1-source, Q1-experiment).
- Controlled time stepping can verify x1/x2/x3 and reduced-motion policy independently of capture speed (Q1-playwright).
- A deterministic result and an exact clock do not prove painted contact; inspect actual adjacent video frames (Q1-experiment).
- Compositor and RAF measurements need an explicit common origin. An observed clock marker gives a correspondence, not a visual verdict (Q1-time, Q1-raf).
- Preserve original raw times and failed methods when choosing a better measurement (Q1-experiment).
- The reference videos guide readability; their accelerated speed and 10 fps sampling cannot set our precise beat durations (Q1-reference).

Never alter the capture, shrink the schedule or infer a visual pass solely from the marker.

# Comparative systems research

Access date: 2026-11-05 UTC (local date 2026-11-06 during continuation). Coverage: **34 games**, all five requested groups including regulation/platform policies. This is source-scoped desk research; no human interviews, live accounts or transaction histories were examined. Detailed fields are in [research-matrix.json](research-matrix.json); source URLs, access dates and supported claims are in [sources.json](sources.json).

Every game row includes core loop, session estimate, battle, stats/formula style, roles, team/positions, skill resources, slots, tiers/colour treatment, affixes/sets, upgrades, duplicates, banners/odds/pity, currencies, daily/weekly, PvE/PvP/social, retention, monetisation, praise and friction. `field_evidence` separates source-supported mechanisms from unverified model synthesis and estimates. A source next to a game does **not** verify every field. Session durations are estimates, exact live numeric tables are deliberately not asserted when unavailable, and player praise/friction is a design hypothesis unless tied to the RPGFan review or a developer's explicit feedback report. Current popularity, retention and market prices were not measured.

Research focuses on mechanisms and causal risks, not cloning. No source art was downloaded, no reference art went to a generator, and no names, icons, layouts or exact numerical tables from other games enter Lantern Cat Café. Names below identify research subjects only. Original design choices and numbers live in PATTERNS.md and DESIGN.md.

## What the comparison does and does not show

Squad games converge on role clarity, collection, build targets and recurring goals. Their acquisition systems do not justify making every system random. Deck builders show that a small readable decision space and previewed consequences can produce depth without dozens of stats. Classic RPGs show why the player needs to understand turn order, positional legality and windows of opportunity. Our conclusion is a compact round-based campaign plus deterministic equipment goals and optional earned discovery, rather than introducing an action-time scheduler.

The on-chain cases identify **mechanisms that may support collectible usefulness**, not evidence of guaranteed price preservation: finite issuance, edition disclosure, actual play utility, access without an initial purchase, transparent circulation accounting, and sinks that do not require perpetual player growth. Issuance above consumption, reward-production assets, bots and dependency on new entrants are plausible failure pressures. Axie's own historical imbalance disclosure is a measured issuer statement; Crabada's closure is a reported event. Neither proves a universal one-factor causal theory of market collapse. Fixed supply cannot create demand or guarantee value.

Lantern Cat Café therefore separates its unlimited playable content from the future scarce collectible identity. The deterministic recipe uses service/quest/boss-earned resources; no random pull is necessary to earn design eligibility. The present code simulates caps, and the existing chain contract does not enforce them. These boundaries are part of the design, not footnotes claiming a production economy.

| Google Play | Payments policy requires purchased randomized-item odds in advance near purchase; other financial/gambling policies remain separate. | No purchase path and no external checkout; make conditional odds and pity visible. | [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en) |

**Required guardrail:** gacha uses only free/earned non-transferable points; no purchase path; exact odds in data and UI; visible pity; random draws never directly yield a tradeable NFT. Future tradeable Relics are restricted to deterministic skill/effort paths with caps, Fuji only. No fungible asset, mainnet or custom L1. Paid or tradeable-draw variants are out of scope and **need legal review**. This research does not certify global legality or contest acceptance.

## Source audit corrections and limitations

The continuation audit replaced an unrelated Hearthstone expansion link with the official duplicate-protection patch notes, replaced a blocked Gods Unchained page with publisher/platform help, used the direct Google Payments policy, and corrected Splinterlands' exception for uncapped current soulbound reward cards. Honkai: Star Rail's social field was corrected to friend support, not a generic guild assertion. Exact guide dates and historical scope remain visible in sources.json.

Mechanisms from all requested titles are represented; not every formula, current live banner, player sentiment or colour code is independently verified. This is intentional evidence honesty, not a claim that generic game knowledge is equivalent to a live audit. The strongest evidence informs the actual decisions: publisher odds, duplicate protection, equipment slot/set mechanics, issuer inflation statements, authoritative legal texts and local contract code. No market valuation recommendation is made.

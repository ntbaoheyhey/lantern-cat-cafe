A healthy-looking first year can hide a stopped economy

360 five-year stress runs: six policies,four adoption assumptions,three player mixes,five fresh seeds. The original 52-week acceptance is retained separately.

Production calibration: NOT MET. Routine rewards remain disabled. These are simulated behavioral assumptions.

Three separate checks

Flow: cumulative claims/burn≤2 and farmer rewards/hour≤3×casual. Utility:75% of casuals can save2 by week13 and50% can save10 by week26. These are declared design targets, not measured preferences. Tail: each of the final four seasons has≥1 YARN of routine rewards and≥1 YARN burned across the entire cohort. Passing flow with zero future activity cannot pass all checks.

Saving estimates exclude grants and resale income; they assume saving for one feature with no other spending. Rename2 is unshipped. Epic seal10 also needs an authoritative item. No game progress requires either purchase.

What the model can and cannot establish

A fixed cohort eventually stops needing duplicate items. More eligible drops cannot create buyers. The carry50 variant tests whether unused,expired budget credit improves continuity without counting the same burn twice. It still obeys the deployed cap, including the permanent zero-cap state. Old unused allocations may expire; existing signed liabilities may never be reused.

Retention is assumed perfect across five years. There are no arrivals, new collections, promotional purchases or investor-funded demand. Source supply is conditional, and bot labels are known perfectly in the model. Real demand and authority remain unproven. Allocation unlocks are separate accounting ceilings, not money injected into the game market or enforced vesting.
